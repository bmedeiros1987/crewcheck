# Paid-period cancellation policy — isolated preparation, HOLD

Product decision: stop renewal on cancellation, retain access through an established paid period, and reconcile refunds separately. This prospective product choice does not establish historical accepted terms or payment coverage.

`createPaidCancellationPolicy` accepts only a trusted injected server coverage reader and clock. It requires exact owner/public identity, provider, subscription and payment binding; an identified/versioned authoritative source; actual paid bounds; fresh evidence; confirmed stopped renewal and no unresolved refund. Trial/grace/status, expiry alone, a received payment date or the historical 31/366-day calculation do not qualify. Unknown, conflicting or unavailable evidence returns reconciliation required with no access mutation. Different existing/paid ends require separate reconciliation; cancellation cannot silently grant or extend access. Exact expiry returns paid-period-ended. The trusted clock is read again after the asynchronous read, so evidence cannot outlive its validity while awaiting it.

This module has no route, network transport, database writer, token persistence or timer. **No real authoritative coverage adapter is configured or implemented.** The existing cancellation helper and Asaas cancellation webhook retain their previous behavior and inconsistency; this isolated policy does not fix the live flow. Google authoritative expiry alone is also insufficient to prove paid rather than trial coverage. Provider rules and effective commercial terms must be reconciled before operational wiring.

The selected input is copied before awaiting evidence. This protects object binding, not session revocation or A→B→A. A future caller must revalidate live account authority, current coverage/refund state and the period inside the same entitlement transaction. A returned decision is neither a grant capability nor permission to write outside that transaction.

Synthetic regression covers 26 cases: Asaas and Google positive contracts, expiry, account/provider/subscription/payment mismatch, non-paid/legacy/unversioned evidence, pending/completed/partial/unknown refund, unconfirmed renewal, stale/future/malformed evidence, conflicting period, future paid start, unavailable reader, mutated input identity and clock advancement during await. No real provider, user receipt, payment, cancellation, schema or storage is touched. Independent read-only review found the stale-clock issue; the revised implementation adds post-await clock validation and two boundary counterexamples. Full Android/browser, actual SQL integration and remote CI are not established by this pure policy regression.

Remaining gates: authoritative payment-specific commercial coverage source, actual session/transaction integration, cancellation webhook/direct-route consistency, refund/chargeback policy, exact final review/CI. Production and PR #1342 remain HOLD. No changes to existing terms, production, retention or automatic recovery.

## Authoritative-source investigation and transactional assessment

The [Google Orders resource](https://developers.google.com/android-publisher/api-ref/rest/v3/orders#SubscriptionDetails) documents servicePeriodStartTime/servicePeriodEndTime as the period funded by an order, explicitly an accounting snapshot, not current entitlement. It instructs using purchases.subscriptionsv2.get for the current end. Order line totals and offer phase can distinguish a priced period from a free trial, but neither a positive order nor its accounting end alone establishes current access after refund, replacement, revocation or deferred billing. No API is called here, no scope/quota access is assumed, and no adapter promotes these fields to operational proof. Google also [requires preserving a canceled free trial through its trial end and grace access where configured](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/subscriptions); this paid-only assessment never revokes those separate entitlements.

The [Asaas subscription FAQ](https://docs.asaas.com/docs/faq-assinaturas) distinguishes recurring charge creation from payment confirmation and explains nextDueDate as charge scheduling. The [single-payment response](https://docs.asaas.com/reference/recuperar-uma-unica-cobranca) provides financial status/payment dates, not a CrewCheck contractual service-period mapping. No reviewed local payment-to-service-period ledger or equivalent accepted contract was found in this branch. An anniversary/competence/timezone mapping cannot be invented from receipt dates, nextDueDate or the legacy 31/366 formula. This is the concrete remaining source dependency.

`createPaidCancellationAssessment` now composes the pure policy with the existing branded transaction client and locked profile/subscription read. It derives account identity and payment binding from locked canonical state, invokes a trusted *local* reader on that same client, and assesses only an existing active matching paid plan. Missing funding metadata, mismatched/recreated accounts and trial/grace/free/lifetime states do not promote access. The reader must not perform provider I/O inside the retriable transaction. No real reader is configured, no evidence is persisted, no renewal is requested and **no subscription/profile write is performed**. This is transactional read/decision preparation, not completed write integration or a repaired cancellation endpoint/webhook.

Fifteen controlled-SQL cases exercise the actual transaction/profile-lock helpers, both provider bindings, trial/grace/pending/refund/canceled exclusions, free/lifetime preservation, absent funding, mismatched payment, pending refund, recreated identity, rollback and escaped-client rejection. These are not MySQL concurrency, physical-device or real-provider tests. Final atomic event/access application still depends on a trustworthy versioned local coverage record and applicable provider rules; no migration or retention period is introduced.

## Google accounting/current cross-check and unresolved Asaas boundary

`createGooglePaidPeriodObservation` reuses the existing current-subscription reader with injected mock transport and cross-checks one order against its owner-bound token, current successful order and product. The conservative supported subset is a processed, positive-price, single base-phase item with explicit valid UTC millisecond accounting bounds. Other phases, multiple items, pending/refunded/partial-refund orders, malformed or mismatched bindings remain unconfirmed. It compares accounting end to the current subscription end and explicitly returns atomicProviderSnapshot=false, orderingEstablished=false and entitlementDecision=not_made. Equal ends do not prove no intervening revocation, nor freshness/atomicity. No raw token, amount, identity, address or response is returned or persisted. No Orders permission, quota, cost or live access is assumed. This observation is intentionally **not** wired into the coverage reader or transaction assessment.

Nineteen synthetic cases use the real current reader and verify paid-base observation, deferred/current-end divergence, all pending/refund states, wrong token/order/product/owner, extra items, trial/intro/proration/ambiguous phases, zero/invalid money and invalid date. Strict UTC millisecond support is a conservative implementation subset, not a claim that Google only accepts that timestamp format.

Source-level Asaas checkout inspection confirms only firstDue (current time plus seven days), MONTHLY/YEARLY charge cycle and plan description are created. No authoritative service start/end record is generated there. Payment-to-subscription association can be verified from the payment subscription field, but it does not supply service coverage. Before implementing operational Asaas coverage, recover an existing accepted contract/ledger defining the service interval or approve a prospective versioned commercial rule addressing: start anchor (due date, payment confirmation or competence), calendar-month/year boundary including end-of-month/leap-year handling, timezone, and late/early payment/overlap behavior. A new rule cannot retroactively prove historical paid bounds. Until this source exists, no paid-coverage adapter, backfill or cancellation access write is justified.

## Confirmed late-payment/reference decision; no duration chosen

Product clarification: a late confirmed payment only funds its associated reference period. If that period has already ended, a new paid purchase is necessary for additional access. It must not roll into a new period based on processing/receipt date. Cancellation/delinquency does not consume an already proven paid remainder. When paid coverage ends, the baseline is free or the account's independently established pre-existing Unlimited grant. A user may eventually purchase additional benefits above Unlimited, but no such benefit tier or price is defined or implemented here. Calendar-anniversary, timezone and annual boundary proposals remain unapproved. Mention of a new 30-day or annual plan does not authorize inventing service bounds for an existing receipt.

`createReferencePaymentPolicy` implements only isolated decisions over an explicit verified paid reference plus trusted account-bound/versioned baseline evidence. It returns reference_access_available, reference_not_started, new_payment_required or reconciliation_required; every result has accessMutation=none. Exact end is expired. It never adds 30/31/365/366 days, issues a charge, overwrites Unlimited, grants a benefit or rewrites a receipt. Unknown payment, source, refund or baseline cannot authorize a downgrade or grant. Coverage and baseline are copied once, the clock is rechecked after both asynchronous readers, and the earlier cancellation policy shares the same evidence checks.

Twenty-four synthetic cases cover current/expired/future and annual explicit references; late payment received today for an expired old reference; exact expiry; free/Unlimited fallback preservation; unknown/non-paid/legacy-derived coverage; wrong payment/provider binding; all unresolved refund states; unversioned/wrong-account/expired baseline evidence; expiry while awaiting baseline and mutable getters. These validate the isolated decision contract, not a live adapter or persistence. Existing cancellation and locked-assessment regressions remain separate. Operational integration still requires real payment-to-reference evidence, current authority and atomic receipt/access writes; legacy route/webhook behavior is unchanged and #1342 remains HOLD.

The output explicitly scopes paymentRequirementScope=paid_reference_only. An expired paid reference is never a command to charge or block an account's Unlimited baseline. Independent read-only review found no new blocker in this narrow policy and highlighted this consumption limit; no real provider, SQL, device or commercial terms were validated by that review.

## Synthetic evidence-binding counterproof

`regression-paid-reference-evidence-binding.mjs` composes the real Asaas read adapter and reference policy around an explicitly fictional reviewed-reference ledger. Twenty-four cases verify exact account/subscription/payment association, reject duplicate/conflicting/missing records and non-paid/refund/deleted/future-date states, preserve an expired reference when its payment arrives today, and remain deterministic without writes. The ledger is a test fixture, not a schema or newly available authoritative source. Both provider reads are mocked and do not become an atomic snapshot. No live proof or SQL concurrency is established.

This fixture bridge deliberately runs outside any transaction. A future real solution must obtain provider observations outside retriable SQL and validate trusted local coverage/evidence inside the atomic owner/event/access transaction. Copying the fixture's mocked provider callback into `readLockedVerifiedCoverage` would violate that contract. Independent read-only inspection found no blocking issue in the test-only harness; production adapters and application remain HOLD.
